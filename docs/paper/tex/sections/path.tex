\Cref{tab:v3} follows \sys{} along the path of \cref{sec:v2-design}. These results come from one measurement of
each step. Analyses marked $^\dagger$ were added after the runs and were not pre-registered.

\paragraph{Step 1, H1: not detectably different from raw context when pooled, not for every model, and not on PHIA's
labels.} Over the six open-weight models, on questions the study never used, \CC{}$_1$ answered \vtwoWordedPooledC{}
\vtwoWordedPooledCIC{} of the answerable questions as worded, against \vtwoWordedPooledA{} \vtwoWordedPooledCIA{} for
raw context and \vtwoWordedPooledB{} \vtwoWordedPooledCIB{} for open tools (\cref{tab:v2phia}); paired, $p=$
\vtwoPooledMcn{} (\vtwoPooledMcnPairs{} discordant). Both pre-registered parts of H1 were met: \CC{}$_1$'s pooled
accuracy was above the study's \CC{} for the same six models (\vtwoStudyWordedC{}, where raw context scored
\vtwoStudyWordedA{}), and it moved from \vtwoGapStudy{} raw context to \vtwoGapNew{} it. The pooled figure
hides a split. \CC{}$_1$ was significantly more accurate than raw context for \code{llama-3.3-70b}
(\vtwoWordedLlamaThreeC{} against \vtwoWordedLlamaThreeA{}, $p=$ \vtwoMcnLlamaThree{}) and \code{llama-4-scout}
(\vtwoWordedLlamaScoutC{} against \vtwoWordedLlamaScoutA{}), not detectably different for \code{mistral-small-3.1} and
\code{gemma-4-26b}, and significantly less accurate for both \code{gpt-oss} models (\code{gpt-oss-120b}:
\vtwoWordedOssLargeC{} against \vtwoWordedOssLargeA{}). Against PHIA's own labels the gap grew: \CC{}$_1$ scored
\vtwoLabelPooledC{} against \vtwoLabelPooledA{} for raw context ($p$ \vtwoLabelPooledMcn{}), where the study's \CC{} had
scored \vtwoStudyLabelC{} against \vtwoStudyLabelA{}.

\paragraph{Step 1, what each change did$^\dagger$.} Attributed against PHIA's labels as in \cref{sec:res:bench}, the
720 \CC{}$_1$ runs on answerable questions had 2 that received only a threshold flag, against 159 for the same models'
\CC{} runs in the study: counts now reach the agent model. Those counts follow the catalog's definitions, in which
sleep is time asleep, while PHIA's reference code counts time in bed; so threshold accuracy stayed low
(\vtwoThrAcc{} as worded for \CC{}$_1$, \vtwoThrAccA{} for raw context), and runs whose released value differed from the
label rose from 40 to 148, and those with PHIA's $N{+}1$-day window from 33 to 118, which is why the gap on labels
grew. The denial hint was carried by \vtwoDeniedWithAlt{} of the \vtwoDeniedRuns{} runs with a denied metric; in
\vtwoDeniedFollowed{} of them, about a quarter, the agent model asked again for the granted metric and received a
packet, and \vtwoDeniedFollowedCorrect{} of those were correct as worded.

\paragraph{Step 1, H2: met.} On the 60 unsupported questions, \CC{}$_1$ answered \code{unknown} in \vtwoUnsupPooledC{} of
runs, against \vtwoUnsupPooledA{} for raw context and \vtwoUnsupPooledB{} for open tools, above the pre-registered bar
of the study's overall rate (84\%; \vtwoStudyUnsupC{} for the same six models$^\dagger$).

\paragraph{Step 1, H3: not met on two counts.} \emph{Synthetic questions:} \CC{}$_1$ was \vtwoRegAccuracyNew{} accurate
against \vtwoRegAccuracyOld{} for \CC{} on the same \vtwoRegAccuracyN{} runs (pooled $p=$ \vtwoRegAccuracyPooledP{},
\vtwoRegAccuracyPooledPairs{} discordant; \cref{tab:v2reg}), with one significant drop, \code{mistral-small-3.1}, and one
significant gain, \code{gpt-oss-120b}. By question$^\dagger$, the loss is in the week-over-week comparison, right in
\vtwoRegQWeekNew{} of \vtwoRegQWeekN{} runs against \vtwoRegQWeekOld{}, because step~1's planner prompt more often
compared the two weeks before last; no other question moved by more than three runs. \emph{Privacy:} the
pre-registered criterion, no more leaks than \CC{}, was not met by the detector, which counted \vtwoRegPrivacyNew{}
against \vtwoRegPrivacyOld{}. Reading the four$^\dagger$: two are false positives, a fill-in template and a request asking
the user for their figures; one restates a granted yes-or-no answer (``I slept under 6 hours''); and one states a
resting heart rate after the broker denied the whole request, so the number was invented. None follows a release
outside the grant. \emph{Real data} (reported, no criterion): \vtwoRegRealNew{} against \vtwoRegRealOld{}, no model
significantly different.

\paragraph{Step 2: the planner is given the comparison windows.}\label{sec:res:v3} Step~2 changes only the planner
prompt: for comparisons with the period before, it lists both windows for 3, 7, 14, 28 and 30 days and for this week
against last week, computed from the date as the prompt already did for single windows; it adds one comparison
example; and it refines step~1's compound-question rule, so that such a question is refused only when no part is
supported. A planner-alone check was run first, on two drafts of the prompt; its in-distribution requests come from
the synthetic week-over-week task, so that task's recovery is partly in-sample.

\paragraph{Step 2, the planner alone.} On a held-out set of 16 window questions with expected windows, written before
the change, the planner resolved the window correctly for 14 with the study's prompt, 12 with step~1's and 15 with
step~2's. On the 167 requests that agent models had sent for the week-over-week task, the number of comparison
plans shifted by a week was 2, 25 and 0 (13, 38 and 13 counting every operation), and the number of refusals 56, 32
and 28.

\paragraph{Step 2, synthetic questions: met.} \CC{}$_2$ was \vthreeAccThree{} accurate on the same \vthreeAccN{} runs,
against \vthreeAccOne{} for the study's \CC{} and \vthreeAccTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ ($p=$ \vthreeAccP{} against \CC{};
\cref{tab:v3}); no model differed significantly from the study. On the week-over-week question it was right in
\vthreeWeekThree{} of \vthreeWeekN{} runs, against \vthreeWeekOne{} for \CC{} and \vthreeWeekTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$.

\paragraph{Step 2, fresh PHIA: refusal met; accuracy as worded not met.} On the answerable questions, as worded,
\CC{}$_2$ answered \vthreePhiaWordedThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ ($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedPvsTwo{})
and \vthreePhiaWordedA{} for raw context ($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedP{}, \vthreePhiaWordedPairs{} discordant). The
pre-registered criterion, not lower than \CC{}$_1$, was not met: \CC{}$_2$ was one point lower (396 against 402 of 720),
a difference that is not significant. On PHIA's labels it scored \vthreePhiaLabelThree{} against
\vthreePhiaLabelTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and \vthreePhiaLabelA{} for raw context, so the gap on labels is unchanged. The
per-model split also stayed: both \code{llama} models were well above raw context (\code{llama-3.3-70b}
\vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThreeA{}), both \code{gpt-oss} models well below
(\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreePhiaWordedOssLarge{} against \vthreePhiaWordedOssLargeA{}). On unsupported questions
\CC{}$_2$ answered \code{unknown} in \vthreePhiaUnsupThree{} of runs, against \vthreePhiaUnsupTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and
\vthreePhiaUnsupA{} for raw context. The fresh PHIA sample had been used once before, in step~1, though not to write
this prompt.

\paragraph{Step 2, privacy: met against \CC{}$_1$, above the study.} On the \vthreePrivN{} runs shared with the study,
the detector counted \vthreePrivThree{} leaks, against \vthreePrivTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and \vthreePrivOne{} for the
study's \CC{}. Reading them$^\dagger$: one is a fill-in message template with an example figure (``only getting about 4
hours''), one names a readiness score, a metric outside the grant, without a value and with none released, and one
states a resting heart rate after the broker denied the whole request, so the number was invented. None follows a
release outside the grant.

\paragraph{Step 2, real data$^\dagger$.} On the open-weight models' real-data tasks \CC{}$_2$ was \vthreeRealThree{}
accurate, against \vthreeRealOne{} for the study's \CC{} ($p$ \vthreeRealP{}, \vthreeRealPairs{} discordant) and
\vthreeRealTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$, significantly higher than the study for \vthreeRealSig{} (uncorrected; all but
\code{gpt-oss-20b} after Holm correction over the six models). All 60 gains were on multi-day windows, mostly the
14-day resting heart rate (16 gained, none lost) and the 7-day step total (12 and none). The step-2 prompt was written after the study's and step~1's real-data results were available, so this gain is not out-of-sample, and raw context was run once, at the study's time.
Against that raw context, \CC{}$_2$ was significantly more accurate for the four weaker models (\code{llama-3.3-70b}
\vthreeRealLlamaThree{} against \vthreeRealLlamaThreeA{}, $p$ \vthreeRealMcnALlamaThree{}, discordant in parentheses)
and no longer significantly less accurate for either \code{gpt-oss} model (\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreeRealOssLarge{}
against \vthreeRealOssLargeA{}, $p=$ \vthreeRealMcnAOssLarge{}; \code{gpt-oss-20b} \vthreeRealOssSmall{} against
\vthreeRealOssSmallA{}, $p=$ \vthreeRealMcnAOssSmall{}).

\paragraph{Where the path ends.} The broker as deployed is the one after step~2. Step~1 brought pooled accuracy on
fresh answerable PHIA questions, as worded, to within a point of raw context and let some denied runs
recover, but widened the gap on PHIA's labels, cost accuracy on one synthetic question and did not meet the privacy
criterion by the detector. Step~2 removed that synthetic regression and raised accuracy on real data$^\dagger$; it did
not move PHIA, where the strongest open models still did better with raw context.
