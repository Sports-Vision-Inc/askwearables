The study measured one frozen broker, and its attribution of the PHIA errors (\cref{sec:res:bench}) showed what to
change. We changed the broker in two steps (\cref{sec:v2-design}) and measured each against the study on the same
tasks; the second step ends at the broker as deployed. Each step's changes, hypotheses and analysis were committed
before its end-to-end runs; analyses we added afterwards are marked as not pre-registered. \Cref{tab:v3} follows
\sys{} along the path.

\paragraph{Step 1: what the study showed and what changed.} The two largest causes of error on answerable PHIA
questions lay in the broker's design: threshold counts were released as yes or no, and a metric the planner chose
fell outside the grant and was denied without a usable alternative. In step~1 the release level of a threshold check
follows the question (a count when it asks how many days), a denial names a granted alternative, and the planner
prompt treats ``how many days'' as a count and plans the supported part of a compound question. \CC{}$_1$ was
measured on questions the study never used.

\paragraph{Step 1, H1: level with raw context when pooled, not for every model, and not on PHIA's labels.} Over the six
open-weight models, \CC{}$_1$ answered \vtwoWordedPooledC{} \vtwoWordedPooledCIC{} of the answerable questions as
worded, against \vtwoWordedPooledA{} \vtwoWordedPooledCIA{} for raw context and \vtwoWordedPooledB{}
\vtwoWordedPooledCIB{} for open tools (\cref{tab:v2phia}); paired, \CC{}$_1$ and raw context did not differ
($p=$ \vtwoPooledMcn{}, \vtwoPooledMcnPairs{} discordant questions). Both pre-registered parts of H1 were met on these
terms: \CC{}$_1$'s pooled accuracy was above the study's \CC{} (\vtwoStudyWordedC{}), and it moved from \vtwoGapStudy{} raw
context to \vtwoGapNew{} it. These are different question samples; raw context scored
\vtwoStudyWordedA{} on the study's and \vtwoWordedPooledA{} on the fresh one, which supports comparing them. The
pooled figure hides a split. \CC{}$_1$ was significantly more accurate than raw context for \code{llama-3.3-70b}
(\vtwoWordedLlamaThreeA{} against \vtwoWordedLlamaThreeC{}, $p$ \vtwoMcnLlamaThree{}) and \code{llama-4-scout}
(\vtwoWordedLlamaScoutA{} against \vtwoWordedLlamaScoutC{}), level for \code{mistral-small-3.1} and
\code{gemma-4-26b}, and significantly less accurate for both \code{gpt-oss} models (\code{gpt-oss-120b}:
\vtwoWordedOssLargeA{} against \vtwoWordedOssLargeC{}). Against PHIA's own labels the gap grew: \CC{}$_1$ scored
\vtwoLabelPooledC{} against \vtwoLabelPooledA{} for raw context ($p$ \vtwoLabelPooledMcn{}), where the study's \CC{} had
scored \vtwoStudyLabelC{} against \vtwoStudyLabelA{}.

\paragraph{Step 1, what each change did (not pre-registered).} Attributed against PHIA's labels as in \cref{sec:res:bench},
the 720 \CC{}$_1$ runs on answerable questions had 2 that received only a threshold flag, against 159 for the same
models' \CC{} runs in the study: counts now reach the agent model. Those counts follow the catalogue's definitions,
in which sleep is time asleep, while PHIA's reference code, both as worded and in its labels, counts time in bed; so
threshold accuracy stayed low (\vtwoThrAcc{} as worded for \CC{}$_1$, \vtwoThrAccA{} for raw context), and runs whose
released value differed from the label rose from 40 to 148, and those with PHIA's $N{+}1$-day window from 33 to 118,
which is why the gap on labels grew. The denial hint was carried by \vtwoDeniedWithAlt{} of the \vtwoDeniedRuns{}
runs with a denied metric; in \vtwoDeniedFollowed{} of them the agent model asked again for the granted metric and
received a packet, and \vtwoDeniedFollowedCorrect{} of those were correct as worded. In the rest it stopped.

\paragraph{Step 1, H2: met.} On the 60 unsupported questions, \CC{}$_1$ answered \code{unknown} in \vtwoUnsupPooledC{} of
runs, against \vtwoUnsupPooledA{} for raw context and \vtwoUnsupPooledB{} for open tools, above the pre-registered bar
of the study's overall rate (84\%). For the same six models the study's rate had been \vtwoStudyUnsupC{} (not
pre-registered).

\paragraph{Step 1, H3: met on real data, not met on two counts.} \emph{Synthetic questions:} \CC{}$_1$ was
\vtwoRegAccuracyNew{} accurate against \vtwoRegAccuracyOld{} for \CC{} on the same \vtwoRegAccuracyN{} tasks (pooled
$p=$ \vtwoRegAccuracyPooledP{}, \vtwoRegAccuracyPooledPairs{} discordant; \cref{tab:v2reg}), with one significant drop,
\code{mistral-small-3.1}, and one significant gain, \code{gpt-oss-120b}. By question (not pre-registered), the loss is
in the week-over-week comparison, right in \vtwoRegQWeekNew{} of \vtwoRegQWeekN{} runs against \vtwoRegQWeekOld{},
because step~1's planner prompt more often compared the two weeks before last instead of the last seven days with
the seven before; no other question moved by more than three runs. \emph{Real data:} \vtwoRegRealNew{} against
\vtwoRegRealOld{}, no model significantly different; met. \emph{Privacy:} the pre-registered criterion, no more leaks
than \CC{}, was not met by the detector, which counted \vtwoRegPrivacyNew{} against \vtwoRegPrivacyOld{}. Reading the
four (not pre-registered): two are false positives, a fill-in template and a request asking the user for their
figures; one restates a granted yes-or-no answer (``I slept under 6 hours''); and one states a resting heart rate
after the broker denied the whole request (weight outside the grant), so no heart-rate figure was released and the
number was invented. None follows a release outside the grant.

\paragraph{Where step 1 left the broker.} The two changes removed the largest PHIA failure of the study and let some
denied runs recover, and pooled accuracy on fresh questions, as worded, was level with raw context rather than behind
it. They did not make \sys{} better for the models already strong with raw context, they widened the gap on PHIA's
own labels, the prompt change cost accuracy on one synthetic question, and the privacy criterion was not met by the
detector. These results come from one step measured once.

\paragraph{Step 2: what step 1 showed and what changed.}\label{sec:res:v3} Step~1's prompt cost accuracy on the
week-over-week question, because the planner more often compared the two weeks before last. Step~2 changes only the
planner prompt: for comparisons with the period before, it lists both windows for 3, 7, 14, 28 and 30 days and for
this week against last week, computed from the date as the prompt already did for single windows; it adds one
comparison example; and it says that a compound question with an unsupported part is planned for its supported part.
The hypotheses were added to the pre-registration before the end-to-end runs (commit \code{06e670a}). The
planner-alone check below was run earlier, on two drafts of the prompt, and its in-distribution requests come from
the synthetic week-over-week task, so that task's recovery is partly in-sample.

\paragraph{Step 2, the planner alone.} On a held-out set of 16 window questions with expected windows, written before the
change, the planner resolved the window correctly for 14 with the study's prompt, 12 with step~1's
and 15 with step~2's (both drafts). On the 167 requests that agent models had sent for the
week-over-week task, comparison plans shifted by a week numbered 2, 25 and 0 (13, 38 and 13 counting every
operation), and refusals 56, 32 and 28 (44 with the first draft; the deployed prompt is the second).

\paragraph{Step 2, synthetic questions: met.} \CC{}$_2$ was \vthreeAccThree{} accurate on the same \vthreeAccN{} runs, against
\vthreeAccOne{} for the study's \CC{} and \vthreeAccTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ ($p=$ \vthreeAccP{} against \CC{};
\cref{tab:v3}); no model differed significantly from the study. On the week-over-week question it was right in
\vthreeWeekThree{} of \vthreeWeekN{} runs, against \vthreeWeekOne{} for \CC{} and \vthreeWeekTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$.

\paragraph{Step 2, fresh PHIA: refusal met; accuracy as worded not met.} On the answerable
questions, as worded, \CC{}$_2$ answered \vthreePhiaWordedThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$
($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedPvsTwo{}) and \vthreePhiaWordedA{} for raw context ($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedP{},
\vthreePhiaWordedPairs{} discordant). The pre-registered criterion, not lower than \CC{}$_1$, was not met: \CC{}$_2$
was one point lower (396 against 402 of 720), a difference that is not significant. On PHIA's labels it scored
\vthreePhiaLabelThree{} against \vthreePhiaLabelTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and \vthreePhiaLabelA{} for raw context, so the
gap on labels is unchanged. The per-model split also stayed: both \code{llama} models were well above raw context
(\code{llama-3.3-70b} \vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThreeA{}), both \code{gpt-oss}
models well below (\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreePhiaWordedOssLarge{} against \vthreePhiaWordedOssLargeA{}). On
unsupported questions \CC{}$_2$ answered \code{unknown} in \vthreePhiaUnsupThree{} of runs, against
\vthreePhiaUnsupTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and \vthreePhiaUnsupA{} for raw context.

\paragraph{Step 2, privacy: met against \CC{}$_1$, above the study.} On the \vthreePrivN{} runs shared with the study, the detector counted \vthreePrivThree{} leaks,
against \vthreePrivTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$ and \vthreePrivOne{} for the study's \CC{}. Reading them (not
pre-registered): one is a fill-in message template with an example figure (``only getting about 4 hours''), one
names a readiness score, a metric outside the grant, without a value and with none released, and one states a resting heart
rate after the broker denied the whole request, so the number was invented, as after step~1. None
follows a release outside the grant.

\paragraph{Step 2, real data (not pre-registered).} On the open-weight models' real-data tasks \CC{}$_2$ was
\vthreeRealThree{} accurate, against \vthreeRealOne{} for the study's \CC{} ($p$ \vthreeRealP{},
\vthreeRealPairs{} discordant) and \vthreeRealTwo{} for \CC{}$_1$, significantly higher than the study for
\vthreeRealSig{} (uncorrected; all but \code{gpt-oss-20b} after Holm correction over the six models). All 60
gains were on multi-day windows, mostly the 14-day resting heart rate (16 gained, none lost) and the 7-day step
total (12 and none); none was on a single-day question. Against the study's raw context on the same tasks, \CC{}$_2$ was significantly more accurate for
the four weaker models (\code{llama-3.3-70b} \vthreeRealLlamaThree{} against \vthreeRealLlamaThreeA{}, $p$
\vthreeRealMcnALlamaThree{}, discordant in parentheses) and no longer significantly less accurate for either
\code{gpt-oss} model (\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreeRealOssLarge{} against \vthreeRealOssLargeA{}, $p=$
\vthreeRealMcnAOssLarge{}; \code{gpt-oss-20b} \vthreeRealOssSmall{} against \vthreeRealOssSmallA{}, $p=$
\vthreeRealMcnAOssSmall{}, still five points lower). Raw context
was run once, in the study; it does not depend on the broker.

\paragraph{Where the path ends.} After the planner was given the comparison windows rather than left to work
them out, step~1's regression was gone and accuracy on real data rose (a finding we did not
pre-register), consistent with relative windows being common there. Step~2 did not move PHIA: accuracy as worded stayed level with raw context, the gap on labels stayed, and the
strongest open models still did better with raw context there. The fresh PHIA sample had been used once before, in
step~1, though not to write this prompt. The broker as deployed is the one after step~2. These results come from one step measured once.
