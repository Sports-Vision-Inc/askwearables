\section{Scope of the Study}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Synthetic cohort.} The accuracy and privacy studies use ten synthetic people whose ground truth is known
by construction. This lets us score every answer against what actually happened rather than against what a device
or the broker reports, and lets us place each data-quality trap deliberately. The cost is realism and size: real
exports have more varied gaps, devices and artifacts than ten seeded profiles, each person carries one trap, each
task was run once per model and condition, and the questions name their windows explicitly. Tasks are clustered
within people, which is why we report a bootstrap over people next to the per-task tests. The results describe these
traps and questions, not the distribution of questions real users ask; the real-data suite (\cref{sec:res:bench})
addresses part of this.

\paragraph{Scoring and broker designed by the same authors.} The reference rules and the broker were written by the
same team, and some definitions coincide. The HRV reference is \emph{flat} for every person without an HRV trap,
while the broker calls a trend only when the slope's $t$ statistic reaches 2.5. ``Sleeping less than usual'' is
defined by quartiles of the prior nights, and the broker compares with an interquartile baseline; \usualLenient{} of
the \usualTasks{} such tasks accept either answer. The planner prompt resolves the windows the questions use
(``ending yesterday''). Each of these favors \CC{} to an unknown degree; the PHIA and real-data suites, whose
references the broker's authors did not define or computed independently, are the check.

\paragraph{Public benchmark.} The planner and compute-engine studies use PHIA's public objective question set and
its released data (commit \code{\phiaCommit}), under its CC BY-NC 4.0 license. The planner was chosen on the
selection sample, its refusal list follows PHIA's unsupported categories, and the directly answerable subsets are
small (\plLlamaThreeNAns{} and \plHeldLlamaThreeNAns{} questions), so agreement figures carry wide uncertainty. The
compute-engine study uses hand-mapped queries and covers the \phiaMapped{} questions inside the operation set.

\paragraph{Models and serving.} We evaluated the \nModels{} models listed in \cref{sec:models}, each through one
provider and with provider-default sampling. Open-weight models served elsewhere, with other quantization or
batching, may behave differently~\citep{yuan2025nondeterminism,he2025nondeterminism}. All runs share one evaluation
date. Privacy runs for the frontier models use \privPeopleOther{} of the \privPeopleOpen{} synthetic people. Repeatability covers two
sleep questions and the open-weight models only.

\paragraph{Leak scoring.} Leaks are scored by deterministic rules over the agent model's final text, with no model as
a judge, corrected after a check against one annotator's labels and not re-validated on a fresh labeled sample
(\cref{sec:res:privacy}).

\paragraph{Baselines and ablations.} \CA{} and \CB{} represent the two prevailing designs, not every possible one. We
did not compare against PHIA's code-writing agent, a model-mediated minimizer~\citep{bagdasarian2024airgap}, a
deterministic tool gate without the metric layer~\citep{shi2025progent}, \CC{} with an oracle planner, or \CA{} with
self-consistency at matched compute~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}; each would separate a different part of
\sys{}'s effect.
