The second iteration's prompt cost accuracy on the week-over-week question, because the planner more often compared
the two weeks before last. The third revision changes only the planner prompt: for comparisons with the period
before, it lists both windows for 3, 7, 14, 28 and 30 days and for this week against last week, computed from the
date as the prompt already did for single windows; it adds one comparison example; and it says that a compound
question with an unsupported part is planned for its supported part. The hypotheses were added to the
pre-registration before the end-to-end runs (commit \code{06e670a}). The planner-alone check below was run
earlier, on two drafts of the prompt, and its in-distribution requests come from the synthetic week-over-week task,
so that task's recovery is partly in-sample.

\paragraph{The planner alone.} On a held-out set of 16 window questions with expected windows, written before the
change, the planner resolved the window correctly for 14 with the study's prompt, 12 with the second iteration's
and 15 with the third revision's (both drafts). On the 167 requests that agent models had sent for the
week-over-week task, comparison plans shifted by a week numbered 2, 25 and 0 (13, 38 and 13 counting every
operation), and refusals 56, 32 and 28 (44 with the first draft; the deployed prompt is the second).

\paragraph{Synthetic questions: met.} \CC{}$_3$ was \vthreeAccThree{} accurate on the same \vthreeAccN{} runs, against
\vthreeAccOne{} for the study's \CC{} and \vthreeAccTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$ ($p=$ \vthreeAccP{} against \CC{};
\cref{tab:v3}); no model differed significantly from the study. On the week-over-week question it was right in
\vthreeWeekThree{} of \vthreeWeekN{} runs, against \vthreeWeekOne{} for \CC{} and \vthreeWeekTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$.

\paragraph{Fresh PHIA: refusal met; accuracy as worded not met.} On the answerable
questions, as worded, \CC{}$_3$ answered \vthreePhiaWordedThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$
($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedPvsTwo{}) and \vthreePhiaWordedA{} for raw context ($p=$ \vthreePhiaWordedP{},
\vthreePhiaWordedPairs{} discordant). The pre-registered criterion, not lower than \CC{}$_2$, was not met: \CC{}$_3$
was one point lower (396 against 402 of 720), a difference that is not significant. On PHIA's labels it scored
\vthreePhiaLabelThree{} against \vthreePhiaLabelTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$ and \vthreePhiaLabelA{} for raw context, so the
gap on labels is unchanged. The per-model split also stayed: both \code{llama} models were well above raw context
(\code{llama-3.3-70b} \vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThree{} against \vthreePhiaWordedLlamaThreeA{}), both \code{gpt-oss}
models well below (\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreePhiaWordedOssLarge{} against \vthreePhiaWordedOssLargeA{}). On
unsupported questions \CC{}$_3$ answered \code{unknown} in \vthreePhiaUnsupThree{} of runs, against
\vthreePhiaUnsupTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$ and \vthreePhiaUnsupA{} for raw context.

\paragraph{Privacy: met against \CC{}$_2$, above the study.} On the \vthreePrivN{} runs shared with the study, the detector counted \vthreePrivThree{} leaks,
against \vthreePrivTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$ and \vthreePrivOne{} for the study's \CC{}. Reading them (not
pre-registered): one is a fill-in message template with an example figure (``only getting about 4 hours''), one
names a readiness score, a metric outside the grant, without a value and with none released, and one states a resting heart
rate after the broker denied the whole request, so the number was invented, as in the second iteration. None
follows a release outside the grant.

\paragraph{Real data (not pre-registered).} On the open-weight models' real-data tasks \CC{}$_3$ was
\vthreeRealThree{} accurate, against \vthreeRealOne{} for the study's \CC{} ($p$ \vthreeRealP{},
\vthreeRealPairs{} discordant) and \vthreeRealTwo{} for \CC{}$_2$, significantly higher than the study for
\vthreeRealSig{} (uncorrected; all but \code{gpt-oss-20b} after Holm correction over the six models). All 60
gains were on multi-day windows, mostly the 14-day resting heart rate (16 gained, none lost) and the 7-day step
total (12 and none); none was on a single-day question. Against the study's raw context on the same tasks, \CC{}$_3$ was significantly more accurate for
the four weaker models (\code{llama-3.3-70b} \vthreeRealLlamaThree{} against \vthreeRealLlamaThreeA{}, $p$
\vthreeRealMcnALlamaThree{}, discordant in brackets) and no longer significantly less accurate for either
\code{gpt-oss} model (\code{gpt-oss-120b} \vthreeRealOssLarge{} against \vthreeRealOssLargeA{}, $p=$
\vthreeRealMcnAOssLarge{}; \code{gpt-oss-20b} \vthreeRealOssSmall{} against \vthreeRealOssSmallA{}, $p=$
\vthreeRealMcnAOssSmall{}, still five points lower). Raw context
was run once, in the study; it does not depend on the broker.

\paragraph{Reading the third revision.} After the planner was given the comparison windows rather than left to work
them out, the second iteration's regression was gone and accuracy on real data rose (a finding we did not
pre-register), consistent with relative windows being common there. It did not move PHIA: accuracy as worded stayed level with raw context, the gap on labels stayed, and the
strongest open models still did better with raw context there. The fresh PHIA sample had been used once before, in
the second iteration, though not to write this prompt. These results come from one revision measured once.
