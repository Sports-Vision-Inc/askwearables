\begin{abstract}
\noindent
AI agents asked about a person's wearable data either read the whole record or fetch it through unrestricted tools,
leaving computation and disclosure to the model. We present \sys{}, a broker in which an LLM planner that never sees
the stored record turns each question into a typed query over tested code, a policy gate releases the coarsest of
four levels that a per-agent, per-purpose grant allows, and the agent receives a short packet with a hash-chained
receipt. Holding the agent model fixed (plus a fixed planner model), we compare \sys{} with raw context and open
tools across \nModels{} models on \nTasks{} questions over a synthetic cohort with known ground truth and realistic
data-quality traps. With \sys{}, accuracy ranged from \accCMin{} to \accCMax{}, against \accAMin{} to \accAMax{} with
raw context and \accBMin{} to \accBMax{} with open tools; the pooled gain over raw context was \bootPooledCAEst{}
points (\bootPooledCACI{}), about 10 excluding a trend question whose reference matches the broker's trend rule.
Accuracy rose significantly for \nSigCA{} of \nTestedCA{} models and fell for none. Raw context put \expRatioAC{}
times as much of the record in front of the model. \sys{} leaked in 1 of 300 privacy runs (raw context
\leakPooledA{}, open tools \leakPooledB{}); in three of four scenarios the requested data lay outside the grant and
were denied by construction, and the one leak, an invented figure, came in the fourth (1 of 75). On PHIA, \sys{}
answered \code{unknown} to \phiaUnsupPooledC{} of out-of-scope questions (raw context \phiaUnsupPooledA{}; some were
computable from the export) but was less accurate on answerable ones (\phiaWordedPooledC{} against
\phiaWordedPooledA{}). Two pre-registered broker changes brought fresh answerable PHIA questions to
\vtwoWordedPooledC{} against \vtwoWordedPooledA{} for raw context, though not for every model.
\end{abstract}
