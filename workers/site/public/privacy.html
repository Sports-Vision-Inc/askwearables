What AskWearables does

AskWearables is a broker between data from wearable devices and AI agents. It stores data you, or an app you use, send to it, and answers an agent's questions with small computed summaries, only within limits you approve. It is for general wellness and personal insight. It does not give medical advice and is not intended to detect, diagnose or treat any condition.

Whose data this covers

  • Owners and direct users who sign in to the console and connect their own agents.
  • End users of developers' apps. When an app built on AskWearables sends us your data, that developer decides why your data is processed, and its own privacy policy also applies to you. [Counsel to confirm the controller and processor roles for this case and the terms developers must accept.]
  • Developers who hold an organization and API keys.

How we use it

  • To answer questions from agents you approved, within the grant you approved: the purpose, the metrics, the level of detail, the date window, the daily limit and the expiry.
  • To compute your personal baselines and data-quality notes, which are compared only with your own history.
  • To keep a tamper-evident record of every release and denial, so you can see what was shared.
  • To secure the service, prevent abuse and enforce rate limits.

We do not sell your data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models. [Legal bases for each use, including the basis for processing health data, to be set out by counsel.]

Who receives your data

  • Agents you approve. They receive only computed summaries within your grant. What an agent, or the company behind it, does with an answer after it is released is governed by that company's terms and policy, not by AskWearables.
  • The developer of an app you use, for data relating to you within that app's grant, plus usage counts that contain no health data.
  • Our infrastructure provider, Cloudflare, which hosts the service, including storage and the AI model that turns a question into a query. Question text is processed on Cloudflare's platform; health data is never sent to the model. [List of subprocessors and locations to be completed.]
  • Authorities, where the law requires it. [Policy on government requests to be added.]

Where it is stored and how it is protected

  • Each user's health data is kept in a separate store, encrypted at rest with a key specific to that user, which is itself encrypted with a master key held as a platform secret.
  • All connections use HTTPS.
  • Access is split across separate services, so the parts reachable from the internet hold no stored health data and no long-lived secrets.
  • [Storage region and international transfer safeguards to be confirmed.]

No system is perfectly secure. We will notify you and the relevant authorities of a breach affecting your data as the law requires. [Notification commitments to be confirmed.]

How long we keep it

  • Health data and account data: until you delete it, or until the developer of the app you use deletes it. [Retention after account inactivity to be decided.]
  • Answers already given to an agent: an encrypted copy is kept for about 24 hours so the agent can fetch it again, then removed.
  • Receipts: kept as a permanent record. When you delete your data, the details in each receipt become unreadable and only hashes remain, so the record that a release happened survives but not what it contained.
  • Short-lived items: agent access tokens expire after one hour, consent links after one hour, and replay-protection records after five minutes.

Your choices and rights

  • See every release and denial in the console's receipt log.
  • Pause all releases at once, or revoke any one agent.
  • Export all your data as a file.
  • Delete everything, which also revokes all grants and keys.

If you use AskWearables through a developer's app, ask that developer first; we will help them meet your request. You may also have rights under the law where you live, such as rights of access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection and portability, and the right to complain to a data protection authority.
